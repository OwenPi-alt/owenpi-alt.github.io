Restaurant Orders & Payments

An order notification needs a delivery path—and a way to know whether it arrived.

Baiweiyun (百味云) · Senior Software Engineer / Technical Team Lead · 2016.10 — 2018.04

Context and constraints

Yunxiaoer and Yunzhanggui connected restaurant orders, payments, and notifications. Each operation crossed business logic, payment channels, and store devices. A failure at any point could leave staff unsure what to do next. The design focused on clear module responsibilities and explicit boundaries between processing an order and confirming its delivery to a device.

Design decisions and implementation

01

Organize modules around business responsibilities

ChallengeOrders, payments, and risk controls were closely connected. Mixing their responsibilities would make changes and integration work harder to contain.

ApproachUsed the order module to coordinate the core workflow, developed the payment gateway, and worked with the risk-control module, keeping business orchestration distinct from external channel integration.

ValueGave each change a clear place in the system, making its impact easier to assess and development and acceptance work easier to coordinate.

02

Give notification delivery its own design

ChallengeCompleting an order did not establish whether the store or its staff had received the notification.

ApproachThe notification path consumes new-order messages through RabbitMQ, prioritizes Netty persistent connections, and uses JPush as a fallback for store printing and staff notifications.

ValueSeparated order processing from device delivery while retaining both an immediate connection and an alternative notification path.

03

Bound retries using application acknowledgments

ChallengeNetwork changes and device availability could leave notifications unconfirmed, while retries from multiple nodes could create duplicates.

ApproachThe notification path uses application acknowledgments to trigger limited retries for unconfirmed orders, with Redis counters restricting repeated JPush notifications across nodes.

ValueMade retry limits and duplicate controls explicit while preserving the distinction between server-side processing and completion at the device.

Engineering value

Established clear boundaries for orders, payments, and notifications, providing a structure for continued development and failure diagnosis.

Validation focus

  • Are successful order processing and confirmed device delivery represented as distinct states?
  • Are retry limits and stopping conditions explicit when a device disconnects or an acknowledgment is missing?
  • Are duplicate notifications controlled when multiple nodes process the same event?
