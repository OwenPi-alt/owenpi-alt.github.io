AIGC Production Workflow

The central question: how should tasks and budgets recover when generation is interrupted?

Personal R&D · AI-assisted · Offline engineering prototype

Context and constraints

Content production involves generation, rework, review, and editing. Tracking the state and cost of repeated attempts is an engineering problem in its own right. This personal prototype uses offline simulations to explore recovery after interruption, submissions with unknown outcomes, and whether a review remains valid after its media or prompt changes.

Design decisions and implementation

01

Persist evidence before advancing state

ChallengeA request can time out after submission. Retrying immediately may execute the same task again.

ApproachStored shot versions, request hashes, attempt records, and audit events in SQLite WAL. When a submission outcome is unknown, the prototype retains the budget reservation and enters recovery or reconciliation.

ValueDistinguishes an absent response from an unsubmitted task and gives recovery a durable record to work from.

02

Make budget limits part of scheduling

ChallengePrompt changes, retries, and rejected media can leave cost reporting behind the actual number of attempts.

ApproachChecked project and episode budgets and reserved funds atomically within a transaction, using integer accounting. Retry counts span versions, and attempts rejected by quality checks retain their simulated costs.

ValueKeeps budgets, versions, and retries under the same rules, so the cost of a production can be traced to its attempts.

03

Track technical quality and content approval separately

ChallengeValid media files can still fail content review, and replacing an asset can invalidate an earlier approval.

ApproachUsed FFmpeg / ffprobe to check dimensions, duration, audio tracks, black frames, and freezes. Content reviews are bound to the request, file, and acceptance context; changes require a new review.

ValueKeeps evidence for technical checks and content decisions separate, with version checks during assembly to prevent reuse of stale approvals.

Engineering value

Organizes a recoverable content-production workflow around durable state, budget constraints, and version evidence, explored through offline simulation.

Validation focus

  • After an interruption, can recovery distinguish submitted tasks, unknown outcomes, and tasks never submitted?
  • Do prompt or media changes correctly affect retry limits and review validity?
  • Do the files, versions, and approvals used in an edit refer to the same valid attempt?

Research scope

An AI-assisted personal engineering prototype covering task records, recovery, technical quality checks, and editing validation. The focus is engineering control of the generation workflow.

Current stage

Offline workflow and mock-provider validation are complete. Real model integration and generated-content quality evaluation remain future integration work; this is not a client production system.
