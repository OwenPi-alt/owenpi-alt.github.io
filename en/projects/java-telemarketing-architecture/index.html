CASE 04 / ENGINEERING IN DETAIL

Outbound Calling Platform Modernization

Service boundaries had to improve alongside lock contention, timeouts, and out-of-memory failures.

Qifu Technology (奇富科技) · Development Lead · 2018.04 — 2026.03

Context and constraints

As manual and automated outbound calling evolved, the monolith encountered lock contention, timeouts, and out-of-memory failures. The work combined service decomposition with specific runtime, downstream-call, and data-access improvements. It also required coordination across scheduling, configuration, and team delivery while leaving room for further business changes.

Design decisions and implementation

01

Decompose services along business responsibilities

ChallengeResponsibilities within the monolith affected one another, allowing pressure in one area to become a wider runtime problem.

ApproachLed the move from a monolith to microservices and introduced XXL-JOB and Apollo for scheduling and configuration. A dedicated outbound validation service handles call restrictions, blocklists, and phone-number and frequency limits.

ValueCreates clearer boundaries between business rules and execution, giving diagnosis and subsequent changes a defined place in the system.

02

Bound resources for parallel downstream calls

ChallengeAgent-facing tag lookups depended on multiple downstream queries. Sequential waiting and individual failures could both affect result assembly.

ApproachThe tag-query path uses a bounded thread pool and CompletableFuture to fetch downstream results in parallel, retaining available information when an individual query fails.

ValueAllows concurrency within explicit resource limits and preserves a path for partial results when a dependency fails.

03

Address performance along specific access paths

ChallengeLock contention, slow queries, and memory pressure overlapped, so a single configuration adjustment could not address every cause.

ApproachTuned JVM settings for concurrent access, improved SQL indexes and Redis caching, and separated hot and cold data to address runtime behavior, query access, and data volume individually.

ValueLinks each intervention to a concrete source of pressure, making its effect and any remaining bottlenecks easier to assess.

Engineering value

Connected architectural change to concrete reliability problems, with explicit service boundaries and specific implementation points to validate.

Validation focus

  • Do business rules, error responses, and restrictions retain their semantics after service decomposition?
  • Do thread usage and partial-result handling behave as intended when downstream services are slow or fail?
  • Are JVM, SQL, cache, and hot/cold data changes evaluated under comparable loads?
